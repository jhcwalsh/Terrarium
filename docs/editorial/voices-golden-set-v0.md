# Quarter one · Year 4 Q3 — the hawkish surprise

**What happened:** the Committee raised to 5.00% against a rule implying 4.75%, deleted its commitment to returning inflation to target, and drew a two-sided dissent. `ε = +0.25`.

**What the voices should do here:** agree, and be wrong together. This is the quarter where consensus is strongest and the reader should feel it — which is what makes the m52 divergence land.

---

## 1 · The Committee `[T]`

> **For release at 2:00 p.m.**
>
> Economic activity has continued to expand at a slowed pace. Job gains have remained solid and the unemployment rate has stayed low. Inflation has moved higher.
>
> The Committee seeks to achieve maximum employment and inflation at the rate of 2 percent over the longer run. In support of these goals, the Committee decided to raise the target range for the federal funds rate to 5.00 to 5.25 percent.
>
> The Committee judges that risks to price stability have become its principal concern. In determining the extent of additional policy firming that may be appropriate, the Committee will take into account the cumulative tightening of monetary policy and the lags with which monetary policy affects economic activity and inflation.
>
> Voting against the action were Ostrander, who preferred a 50 basis point increase, and Bell, who preferred no change at this meeting.

**Notes for the bank.** Passive, unhurried, no colour, no adjectives that carry judgement. It says *slowed*, not *slowing sharply*; *moved higher*, not *jumped*. **The register is achieved by removing, not by adding** — every draft of a Committee string should be checked for a word that expresses feeling and have it taken out. The deletion carries the news; the prose must not compete with it.

---

## 2 · The columnists `[T]` for the first three, `[L]` for Ferrers

> **Halloran, Kestrel Research** — *consensus*
> Twenty-five basis points nobody had, and the deletion is the real signal. Our reading is that the Committee has moved to a meeting-by-meeting stance and will require two consecutive downside prints before pausing. We move our terminal forecast to 5.25% and push the first cut into next year.

> **Vane** — *policy*
> Read the deletion, not the hike. A committee that stops promising a return to target has privately revised its own model, and the twenty-five basis points are almost beside the point. Ostrander wanted fifty and did not get it, which tells you the centre of the room is closer to the hawks than the vote suggests.

> **Quiñones** — *flows*
> Positioning went into this meeting short duration and long the front end, and got paid on both. What interests me more is what did not move: ten-year yields added four basis points against the two-year's eighteen. Someone is still buying the long end, and it is not the people who write about it.

> **Ferrers** — *structural bear, the outlier* `[L]`
> Everyone will spend this week arguing about the terminal rate, which is the wrong argument. Rates at five per cent are not the story. The story is what five per cent does to balance sheets assembled when money was free, and we will not find out for eighteen months, and by then the argument will have moved on again.

**Notes for the bank.** Halloran is the tell: **he restates consensus and adjusts a forecast** — that is the whole voice, and the reader should learn to skim him. Vane and Quiñones read from the latest data with different emphases. Ferrers is right here, eventually, for reasons he cannot evidence — and per the parity rule he must sound exactly the same in worlds where he stays wrong for a decade.

Dispersion this quarter is **narrow**: three of four are arguing about the same number.

---

## 3 · Ruth Calloway, House Economist `[L]`

> **The Committee has stopped pretending it controls the timing**
>
> Strip out the vote and one sentence matters: the removal of the commitment to bring inflation back to target over the medium term. That language survived nineteen meetings, including two the Committee would rather forget, and it has gone in a quarter when the data were bad but not catastrophic.
>
> A rule fitted to this Committee's own behaviour implies 4.75%. They went to 5.00%. The extra quarter point is judgement, and the deleted sentence is where the judgement is written down — they are no longer confident about *when*, and they have decided that saying so is less costly than being caught out again.
>
> Two dissents from opposite directions, the first in eleven meetings, says the room is not agreed. That is usually a marker of a turning point rather than of a trend.
>
> **What I am watching.** Three things, in descending order of how much I would mind being wrong about them. Funding conditions in private credit, where four years of growth was underwritten at a cost of capital that no longer exists and where nobody has yet had to mark it. Dollar funding into year-end, which is the boring risk that occasionally is not boring. And the fiscal path, where the arithmetic has been ignored for long enough to be interesting.
>
> `thesis: inflation is persistent, labour is not cooperating · risk: inflation · r̂*: 1.4% · confidence: 0.82`

**Notes for the bank.** The economist is allowed the sentence a Committee cannot write — *"they are no longer confident about when"* — because attributing motive is what a columnist does and a central bank does not. **The risk book is the honesty mechanism**: three named risks, of which one pays off in five months and two never do. Written before either outcome is known, and it must read identically in a world where none of them happens.

# Quarter two · Year 5 Q2 — the crisis

**What happened:** Calder Bridge gated, high yield widened 187bp in nine sessions, equities reached −24% from the peak. The Committee held and inserted a financial-stability sentence.

**What the voices should do here:** separate. This is where the three failure modes become legible.

---

## 4 · The Committee `[T]` — mandate lag, rendered

> Economic activity has slowed. Inflation has declined.
>
> The Committee decided to maintain the target range for the federal funds rate at 5.00 to 5.25 percent.
>
> **Financial conditions have tightened, and the Committee is attentive to the implications for economic activity and inflation.** The Committee remains strongly committed to returning inflation to its 2 percent objective.
>
> There were no votes against the action.

**Notes for the bank — this is the most important entry in this document.**

The Committee does not mention Calder Bridge. It does not mention private credit, redemption queues, or secondary discounts. It cannot: those are questions about the functioning of a private market, and the Committee may only speak to financial conditions *as they bear on activity and inflation*. So it says the one sentence its mandate permits, and that sentence is the whole acknowledgement.

**Note also what has come back.** The commitment to returning inflation to target — deleted in month 47 — is restored. A committee that has acquired a second problem needs its first commitment back, and the restoration is a bigger tell than the insertion.

**And: no dissents.** Eleven meetings of argument, and the moment something breaks, unanimity. That is what committees do, it needs no commentary, and the artifact should not supply any.

---

## 5 · The columnists `[T]`

> **Halloran, Kestrel Research**
> We are revising our terminal forecast to 5.00% and now expect the first cut this year. The speed of the repricing in credit has changed the calculus, and we would characterise the risks as two-sided for the first time in this cycle.

> **Vane**
> The insertion is the mirror image of November's deletion, and the restored commitment to target is the sentence to read twice. They have a second objective now and they did not choose it.

> **Quiñones**
> Nine sessions, 187 basis points, and no completed secondary trades reported in buyout at all in April. The bid did not fall. It left.

> **Ferrers**
> I have written this column for six years and I would like to record that being right this month is not the same as having been useful for the previous seventy-one.

**Notes for the bank.** Halloran has **turned after the event** and describes it as a revised calculus — this is the herding voice and it must be rendered without editorial comment, because the point is that the reader notices. Quiñones is closest to useful and, per A.8, will be the least quoted. Ferrers's line is the honest one and is available exactly once in a decade.

Dispersion this quarter is **wide**. Four voices, four different subjects.

---

## 6 · Ruth Calloway `[L]` — QUALIFY

> **A second objective, unchosen**
>
> Something has changed and it is not the inflation data, which came in better than anyone expected and will be a footnote by Friday.
>
> In November this column argued the Committee had stopped believing it controlled the timing. That was right and it was also insufficient. The risk I named third in that piece — funding conditions in private credit — is the one that has arrived, and it has arrived faster than the mechanism I described, which was a slow repricing rather than a queue.
>
> The insertion of a financial-conditions sentence is not a pivot. It is an institution acquiring a second problem while still holding the first, and doing so in the only language its mandate allows. Note that the commitment to target, deleted in November, is back. You do not restore a promise unless you are worried about being thought to have abandoned it.
>
> I do not think this is the bottom in credit, and I am aware I said something similar in year three and was wrong.
>
> `risk: inflation → financial_stability · confidence: 0.48 · contradicted_by: [hy +187bp, gating]`

**Notes for the bank.** Three mechanisms in one column, all reusable: **the partial credit** (right about the risk, wrong about the mechanism — the commonest form of being right and it should be the commonest form here); **the institutional read** the Committee cannot write; and **the volunteered prior error**, which is the single behaviour that separates this voice from commentary the reader should distrust.

# What this implies for the build

| Voice | Backend | Bank size | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Committee statement | `[T]` | ~120 clauses | Fully templated. Register is achieved by subtraction |
| Statement diff, dissents | `[T]` | ~60 | Mechanical, high value |
| Halloran, Vane, Quiñones | `[T]` | ~180 | Consensus-tracking voices template well — they are formulaic by design, which is the point |
| Ferrers | `[L]` | — | The outlier resists templating. Six good lines a decade, and they must not repeat |
| Calloway | `[L]` | — | Stateful, revises, admits error. Not templatable and should not be attempted |
| Calloway's risk book | `[T]` | ~40 | The *ledger* is structured data; only the prose around it needs Tier-2 |

**Two findings for the workbench task.**

1. **Three of four columnists template cleanly, and that is not a compromise.** Consensus-hugging voices are formulaic in life; rendering them formulaically is accurate rather than cheap. Only the outlier needs Tier-2, and it needs very little of it.
2. **The Committee needs no LLM at all.** The most-scrutinised artifact in the product is the most templatable, because its register is defined by what it omits. This is worth knowing before anyone budgets Tier-2 for it.
