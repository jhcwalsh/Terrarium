# JST cross-country scoping note

**Date:** 2026-08-13. **Author:** Claude (agent), for owner review. **Status:** DRAFT — design/research only, no compute run, no code changed, no seal touched. Feeds the campaign-4 go/no-go named in `NEXT-STEPS.md` §2-3; decides nothing by itself.

**The question:** is the Jordà–Schularick–Taylor Macrohistory Database viable as REAL cross-country training data for the L3 neural generator — training on all countries at once with a country embedding, generating US-conditioned decades — and what would activating the declared-unavailable `uk` factor block require?

**Bottom line, up front:** JST is real, is already partly in this repo, and is worth more of — but not in the shape the question asks for. It is annual; the platform's neural block generator (L3) trains on monthly factor blocks. Multiplying L3's training corpus with JST therefore either does nothing (used as-is, at annual frequency) or requires temporal disaggregation, which the K3 rule bans as reconstruction. JST's honest, non-reconstructed contribution is to the *annual* climate layer (L1) and to research-only backtests — both of which the platform's own design documents already scoped it for, before this note existed. Separately, and independent of the country-embedding question: JST's license is Creative Commons **NonCommercial**, which the repo's own license registry currently misstates. See §1 and §6.

## 1. What JST is

The Jordà–Schularick–Taylor Macrohistory Database (`www.macrohistory.net`) is an annual macro-financial panel for 18 advanced economies from 1870 to the present, built by Òscar Jordà, Moritz Schularick and Alan M. Taylor and hosted at the MacroFinance & MacroHistory Lab.

- **Coverage:** 18 countries — Australia, Belgium, Canada, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Ireland, Italy, Japan, Netherlands, Norway, Portugal, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, United Kingdom, United States — described by the Lab as ">90% of advanced-economy output and >50% of world output" ([macrohistory.net/database](https://www.macrohistory.net/database/)).
- **Frequency:** annual, 1870–present.
- **Content:** 48 real and nominal variables across real economy (GDP, consumption, investment, unemployment, wages), international (current account, trade, exchange rates, pegs), government finance, money/prices/interest rates, credit (loans to private sector, mortgages), house prices, systemic-crisis dates, rates of return (equity, housing, bonds, bills), and bank balance-sheet ratios ([macrohistory.net/database](https://www.macrohistory.net/database/); [NBER data page](https://www.nber.org/research/data/jorda-schularick-taylor-macrohistory)).
- **Current release:** R.6 (documentation `JST_documentationR6.pdf`, most recently revised 2023 per its hosted timestamp). No R7 was found in this search — UNVERIFIED beyond that; the Lab's own guidance is to cite the consultation date because "statistics are subject to revisions" (paraphrased from [macrohistory.net/database](https://www.macrohistory.net/database/)). No dedicated errata page was located — UNVERIFIED whether revisions are announced anywhere beyond a new release + updated documentation PDF.
- **License — verified and important:** Creative Commons **Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike 4.0 International (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0)**. The Lab states plainly: *"Commercial data providers are... strictly forbidden to integrate all or parts of the dataset into their services and/or resell the data"* ([macrohistory.net/database/licence-terms](https://www.macrohistory.net/database/licence-terms/)). Distribution terms: shareable only for non-commercial purposes and only under the same license (ShareAlike). Citation is required — the 2017 NBER Macroeconomics Annual paper for general use, the 2019 QJE paper for the returns data, the 2021 Review of Economic Studies paper for bank balance-sheet ratios.

**A repo-internal correction this note surfaces regardless of the campaign-4 decision:** `docs/data/LICENSE-REGISTRY.md` line 69 currently lists `jst` among the 52 "FREE" series and defines FREE as *"no licence needed for commercial use"* (line 7). That is incorrect for JST as verified above — JST is free for **research/non-commercial** use, not commercial use. This is worth fixing in that document independent of everything else in this note; see §6.

**Already in this repo, not new:** the platform has a working `jst` connector (`src/ah/data/connectors/jst.py`) and documentation (`docs/data/jst.md`), both live-verified against the same R6 URL this note found: `https://www.macrohistory.net/app/download/9834512469/JSTdatasetR6.dta`, "151 USA rows." `requirements.yaml` already registers eight USA-only annual JST series — `jst.usa_ltrate`, `usa_stir`, `usa_cpi`, `usa_gdp`, `usa_tloans`, `usa_eq_tr`, `usa_housing_tr`, `usa_crisis` — all `frequency: "A"`, `license_tier: FREE`, `priority: P1`, `intake: auto`, `enforce: false` (i.e. registered and fetchable, but not yet gating anything). The connector's own docstring already notes it "keeps all countries in the raw artifact" and filters to USA only in `parse()` — the cross-country data has been sitting in the raw `.dta` file the connector already downloads; nothing about *fetching* the other 17 countries is new engineering.

`Instructions/DN1.1-multiyear-generator-design-note.md` §II already names JST as an input: the L1 climate layer is specified as a "Bayesian state-space (NUTS)... JST macrohistory 1870– (annual) + monthly panel 1926–" fusion (line 53), and `Instructions/STEP2-GENERATOR-PLAN.md` WP2.5 implements that as "mixed-frequency observation fusing annual JST with the monthly panel" — USA-only, as currently registered. `docs/data-requirements-register.md` line 28 separately scopes JST for "held-out-regime backtests (RQ2)." So the *idea* of using JST is not new; what's new in the question this note answers is (a) using the other 17 countries, not just USA, and (b) doing so with a country embedding to train the L3 *neural block generator*, not the L1 climate layer it currently feeds.

## 2. The frequency problem, honestly

JST is annual. The platform's factor panel that trains L3 — the conditional block generator DN-1.1 §II.4 specifies as "L-month joint factor returns, L ∈ {3,...,12}" over "~1,200 monthly obs" (`Instructions/DN1.1-multiyear-generator-design-note.md` line 55) — is monthly, running 1926–present for the core panel (`STEP1-DATA-PLAN.md` line 14). Those two facts do not reconcile by wanting them to.

**Option A — JST feeds a low-frequency conditioning layer; monthly dynamics stay US-trained.** This is what the platform already does, just USA-only. Extending it cross-country means the L1 Bayesian state-space model (NUTS, numpyro) is fit hierarchically over 18 countries × ~150 years ≈ 2,700 country-years of annual observations, with partial pooling across countries and a country-level offset/embedding in the state-space parameters, instead of fitting only on USA's ~150 country-years. This is a real, non-reconstructed data multiplier — **for L1**. It does not touch L3's monthly corpus at all: L3 still trains on the same ~1,200 US monthly observations it always has, because L1's climate state is an *input* to L3's conditioning vector `c`, not a source of additional monthly training examples. Cost: extending L1's numpyro model to a hierarchical multi-country form, plus deciding what a "country embedding" even means inside a Bayesian state-space model (a per-country random effect on the SDE parameters, most naturally) — a modeling project, not a data project, and one that doesn't touch the L3 architecture the question asks about at all.

**Option B — Redefine L3 to run on annual, country-embedded blocks.** Literally training "the L3 neural generator... on all countries at once with a country embedding" as the question frames it would mean giving L3 annual JST blocks (L=1 year, or a short multi-year block) instead of its current monthly blocks. This is not a data-volume fix to the existing L3 — it is a different generator, with a different block length, a different conditioning contract (DN-1.1 §II.4's `c` vector currently carries monthly regime/state/waypoint context), and a different judged output (the sealed battery's monthly and horizon tiers judge monthly statistics — `pre-registration.yaml`, `Instructions/STEP2-GENERATOR-PLAN.md` WP2.2). It would need its own design note, its own bake-off, and its own seal. Cross-country annual JST *is* real data at real volume for this (2,700 country-years vs. 150), but it answers a different question than "does L3 have more training data" — it proposes a new, coarser-grained sibling to L3, closer in spirit to L1.

**Option C — temporal disaggregation of JST's annual series into pseudo-monthly paths, to feed the existing monthly L3.** This is the literal way to make cross-country JST supply *more monthly blocks*, and it is the one to say no to explicitly. Interpolating or otherwise disaggregating an annual observation into twelve monthly ones invents information the annual figure never carried — precisely the shape of the mistake `docs/alternate-histories-audited.md` records under "What this has taught us about method": *"Never train a model on your own reconstructions... reconstructed data may be used to judge, never to teach."* The campaign that produced that rule (the K3 ablation, `CAMPAIGN3-PROMOTION.md`) found the model trained on real months *only* — 57% less data than the model that also saw HAR-backcast reconstructed vol months — won on the tail tier the platform actually judges by. A disaggregated-JST-into-L3 experiment would recreate that exact defect class at 18-country scale: the model would learn the disaggregation method's artifacts, not history. **This option is closed by standing rule, not by this note's opinion; flagging it is the point of writing this section down.**

**Option D — source genuine monthly cross-country data from elsewhere (not JST).** IMF IFS, OECD Main Economic Indicators, or national-central-bank archives carry monthly series for some of these countries over parts of this history and would be real, not reconstructed, if registered as their own vintaged, licensed connectors. This is a legitimate way to get more real monthly cross-country data — but it is not a JST project. It would be its own scoping note, its own set of connectors (per-country, per-agency, each with its own license and QC quirks), and a materially larger intake effort than anything this note is scoping. Named here only so it isn't confused with what JST itself can supply.

**Net:** the literal ask — country-embedded L3 trained cross-country on JST, generating US-conditioned decades — runs straight into the annual/monthly wall. The honest version of "use JST cross-country" is Option A (a real, cheap L1 multiplier) or, at much greater design cost, Option B (a new coarse-grained sibling layer). Option C is not on the table.

## 3. Mapping JST series to the platform's 16 factors

`factors.yaml`'s `active_blocks: [global, us, fx, valuation]` currently seals 16 factors: `global` — `equity_mkt, smb, hml, mom, equity_vol, commodities, ig_spread, hy_spread` (8); `us` — `policy_rate, ust_2y, ust_10y, cpi, hqm_curve, funding_spread` (6); `fx` — `fx_usd` (1); `valuation` — `cape_v` (1). (The `uk` block — `bank_rate, gilt_nominal_10y, gilt_real_10y, rpi, cpi_uk` — is declared but inactive; see §5.)

JST's per-country variable set (from the eight already-registered USA codes plus the wider 48-variable release) maps as follows:

| Factor | JST analogue | Quality of the match |
|---|---|---|
| `equity_mkt` (total return) | `eq_tr` | **Good.** JST's `eq_tr` is documented as a total-return series per country, matching the platform's sealed `total_return` numeraire directly — no excess-return correction needed the way `french.mkt_rf` needs `+ french.rf`. |
| `cpi` (index level) | `cpi` | **Good.** Same construction — a price index level, not a derived rate. |
| `ust_10y` / `hqm_curve` | `ltrate` | **Partial.** `ltrate` is *a* long-term rate per country, but JST does not distinguish a government-bond tenor point (`ust_10y`) from a corporate curve (`hqm_curve`) the way the platform's two US factors do — one JST series would have to stand in for two conceptually distinct US factors, or for whichever a country's own market convention nearest matches. No corporate-bond analogue exists in JST at all. |
| `policy_rate` | `stir` | **Partial, with a known caution already on record.** JST's `stir` is a short-term market interest rate, not necessarily the administered policy rate — precisely the distinction `factors.yaml`'s own `policy_rate` entry warns about for `fred.TB3MS` (a market bill "decouples from the funds rate in exactly the episodes the tail and severe tiers judge"). The same caution likely applies to JST's `stir` and would need per-country checking, not an assumption of equivalence. |
| `ust_2y` | none | **No analogue.** JST carries no distinct short/medium tenor between `stir` and `ltrate`; the platform's `ust_2y` needs curve-interpolation donors (`fred.GS1`/`GS3`) that JST cannot supply. |
| `funding_spread` | none | **No analogue.** JST has no interbank/commercial-paper rate; nothing plays the TED-spread role. |
| `ig_spread` / `hy_spread` | none | **No analogue, as flagged in the prompt.** JST carries `tloans` (loan stock / credit-to-GDP), a *quantity*, not a credit *spread*. There is no corporate bond yield series in JST at all, so neither `ig_spread` (Baa−Aaa) nor `hy_spread` (OAS) has anything to map to. |
| `smb`, `hml`, `mom` | none | **No analogue.** JST reports one aggregate equity return per country; it carries no cross-sectional decomposition (size, value, momentum) at all. |
| `equity_vol` | none | **No analogue, structurally.** Annual data cannot produce a volatility surface or even a meaningful realized-vol estimate at the platform's monthly cadence. |
| `commodities` | none | **No analogue.** JST has no commodity series; this stays exactly as sealed-missing as it already is. |
| `fx_usd` | `exrate` (peg/exchange-rate series) | **Weak/partial.** JST does carry a cross-country exchange-rate and peg-status series — genuinely one of the better cross-country matches structurally — but it is a bilateral rate per country, not a broad trade-weighted USD index, and it is annual where `fx_usd` is monthly. Not a drop-in. |
| `cape_v` | none | **No analogue.** JST has no price/earnings valuation ratio; deriving one would need JST house-price or equity-price *and* an earnings series it doesn't carry. |

**Honest partial-mapping summary:** of the 16 active factors, 2 have good JST analogues (`equity_mkt`, `cpi`), 2 have partial analogues with known construction caveats (`ust_10y`/`hqm_curve` via `ltrate`, `policy_rate` via `stir`), 1 has a weak structural analogue (`fx_usd` via peg/exchange data), and the remaining 11 have none. JST additionally carries dimensions the platform's factor set doesn't have at all today — `housing_tr` (housing total return), `tloans`/credit-to-GDP (a quantity, not the spread factors), and `crisisJST` (a binary systemic-crisis chronology per country) — which are candidates for enriching L1/L2 (the crisis flag in particular could cross-validate the L2 regime skeleton's NBER + rule-based labels across countries) but are not mappings onto any of the 16 sealed factors.

## 4. The country-embedding training design sketch

Sketched here as a design, not a plan to execute — no compute, no code.

**Shape:** an L1-adjacent hierarchical model (numpyro/NUTS, consistent with the existing L1 implementation) trained on 18 countries × ~150 years of annual JST observations, with a per-country learned offset/embedding on the state-space parameters (or, in a simpler first cut, a per-country random effect with partial pooling toward a global prior — the standard hierarchical-Bayes move, and arguably a more honest description than "embedding," which is a neural-net term for what would here be a small set of per-country coefficients). At generation time, conditioning on the "USA" country token (or its fitted offset) produces US-flavored decade-shape priors informed by the pooled cross-country posterior — the actual product-facing claim would be "history from 17 other advanced economies sharpens the US climate prior," not "the model was trained on more US months."

**What the sealed leakage rules require, concretely:**

1. **JST-as-cross-country enters the catalog exactly like every other source.** CLAUDE.md is explicit that `ah/data/` is the only path in: a vintaged Parquet store, `license_tier` (see §1 — this must read `REG` or a new `FREE-NC` distinction, not `FREE`, once corrected), QC rules, and `is_proxy`/splice flags where applicable. JST needs no splicing — it is raw, non-proxy annual data, unlike most of the platform's extended series (`policy_rate`, `ust_2y`, `hqm_curve` etc., which are all spliced/proxy-extended). That is a genuine point in its favor: JST-derived observations, unlike most of what's in `factors.yaml` today, would carry **zero `is_proxy` months**.
2. **Registering the other 17 countries is a small, mechanical `requirements.yaml` change**, not new fetch engineering — the connector (`src/ah/data/connectors/jst.py`) already downloads the full 18-country `.dta` file and only the `parse()` step's `_COUNTRY = "USA"` filter would need to become a per-country dimension. The design question worth flagging: whether country becomes part of `series_id` (e.g. `jst.gbr_eq_tr`, mirroring the existing `jst.usa_eq_tr` pattern — consistent with the platform's `source.code` identity convention) or whether the schema gains a first-class `country` column alongside `(date, value, series_id, vintage)` (`STEP1-DATA-PLAN.md` line 29's canonical observation schema) — the latter is a bigger, more honest change if country-level partial pooling is the actual downstream consumer, since 18× the `series_id` count for the same eight variables is a lot of near-duplicate catalog rows for what is structurally one panel with a country dimension.
3. **A holdout must be freshly defined for this data — the existing one is spent.** CLAUDE.md states plainly: "the holdout has now been spent (WP5.6) — there is nothing left to hold back." That spend was against the US monthly panel and campaign structure. A JST cross-country training exercise, being a genuinely new dataset feeding a genuinely new (or newly extended) model component, needs its **own** pre-registration-style seal: its own train/validation/holdout split (e.g., held-out countries, held-out decades, or the held-out-regime panels `docs/data-requirements-register.md` line 74 already names for RQ2 — "1970s stagflation; 1930s deflation") defined and hashed *before* any fitting, per the same discipline `ah/eval/prereg.py` enforces for the existing campaigns. It would NOT automatically inherit the exhausted US holdout's exemption or its rules; it is a new leakage surface and needs a new guard.
4. **This is additive to L1's existing frozen structure only if scoped as an L1 extension (Option A, §2).** If scoped as a new sibling layer (Option B, §2), it needs its own design note and its own gate, the way L1/L2/L3 each got one in DN-1.1 — not a quiet extension of an existing sealed layer.

## 5. What activating the declared-unavailable `uk` block needs

`factors.yaml`'s `uk` block (`bank_rate, gilt_nominal_10y, gilt_real_10y, rpi, cpi_uk`) is declared in `factor_blocks` but omitted from `active_blocks`, and each of its five entries is `kind: unavailable`, e.g. for `bank_rate`: *"uk block inactive (decision J3, CLOSED-deferred); no Step-1 UK series registered. `Instructions/WP1.12-UK-CONNECTORS.md` scopes the planned `boe.bank_rate` connector."* — repeated per-series for the other four, naming `boe.glc_nominal_10y`, `boe.glc_real_10y`, `ons.rpi`, `ons.cpi`.

Decision `J3` (`governance/decision-register.md` line 72) reads, verbatim from `pre-registration.yaml`'s `decisions:` key: *"UK-domiciled institution twins are blocked until a `block_addition` amendment; the InstitutionProfile interface accommodates them without rework. Same retraining consequence applies."* Status: **CLOSED-deferred**. A related entry, `S2R-FX-NEXT-CAMPAIGN` (line 88), records the owner's 2026-07-31 decision to fold the `fx` block into a later campaign's retrain rather than adding it alone, on the logic that "the next campaign already carries a full L1/L2/L3 retrain for two other reasons... so adding FX there means ONE retrain buys all three" — the same reasoning would apply to `uk`.

**Concretely, activating `uk` needs:**

1. **The four UK connectors `Instructions/WP1.12-UK-CONNECTORS.md` already scopes** — `boe.bank_rate`, `boe.glc_nominal_10y`, `boe.glc_real_10y`, `ons.rpi`, `ons.cpi` (five series; the file was not read in full for this note, so its detailed acceptance criteria are UNVERIFIED here beyond the connector names `factors.yaml` cites). Each needs the platform's standard connector shape: `fetch()`/`parse()` split, a golden-tested parse against a recorded fixture, a `docs/data/<source>.md` entry, and `requirements.yaml` rows with license tier, priority, and QC severity (`STEP1-DATA-PLAN.md` WP1.2's pattern).
2. **A `block_addition` amendment**, the exact mechanism already used for `fx` (AM-2026-08-02, `governance/amendment-log.yaml` line ~1384) and `valuation` (AM-2026-08-02-008). Per the amendment-log's own documentation of the type: *"additive by construction... does NOT invalidate existing single-block thresholds... carries THRESHOLDS ONLY; `pre-registration.yaml`'s `conventions` block must also be edited, by hand and as part of the same amendment, to classify the new block's factors as return-bearing or level."* This needs new per-block thresholds for the five `uk` factors plus new cross-block thresholds for every pair `uk` forms with each already-active block (`global`, `us`, `fx`, `valuation`) — the amendment log shows the `fx` addition alone required naming specific cross-block pairs ("dollar~equity flight interaction, dollar~long-rate differentials"); `uk` would need its own equivalent enumeration.
3. **Owner ratification reopening J3** — it is explicitly `CLOSED-deferred`, not merely pending; someone has to decide to reopen it, the way `S2R-FX-NEXT-CAMPAIGN` reopened the FX deferral.
4. **A full campaign retrain**, not a data-only change. J3's own text says "same retraining consequence applies" (referring to the FX case) — L3 currently generates only currently-active-block factors; making it emit `uk` factors means the training panel, the battery thresholds, and the L3 architecture's output dimensionality all change together. This is the same scale of work as the `fx`/`valuation` block additions already shipped in campaign-2 (which required real registered data, not a shortcut).

**A specific caution this note adds:** JST *does* carry UK observations (annual `ltrate`, `stir`, `cpi`, `eq_tr`, etc. for GBR), and it would be tempting to read that as "UK data we already have." It is not the same data the `uk` block declares. `bank_rate`, `gilt_nominal_10y`/`gilt_real_10y`, `rpi`, and `cpi_uk` are specific, monthly, officially-sourced UK instruments (Bank of England bank rate and gilt curve, ONS RPI and CPI) — JST's UK annual aggregates are neither the same frequency nor the same instruments, and mapping JST's UK rows onto the `uk` block's five factors would repeat exactly the "plausible substitution" CLAUDE.md and `factors.yaml`'s own header explicitly refuse elsewhere ("never inventing a proxy"). JST-UK is at best a low-frequency cross-check once `uk` connectors exist, not a way to skip building them.

## 6. Cost / benefit and recommendation

**What JST cross-country genuinely buys, priced honestly:**

- **Real, non-reconstructed data volume:** ~2,700 country-years of annual observations across up to 48 variables (8 already mapped to US factor analogues), versus ~150 US country-years today. Zero `is_proxy` months — a cleaner provenance profile than most of the platform's existing extended series.
- **Where it actually lands:** L1's annual climate/state-space layer (a real, cheap multiplier there) and research-only uses already scoped in `docs/data-requirements-register.md` — RQ2 held-out-regime backtests, and now, per this note, cross-country crisis-chronology cross-validation for L2. It does **not** multiply L3's monthly training corpus — the actual resource that was scarce in the K3 finding — without banned disaggregation (§2, Option C).
- **What it costs to get there:** the connector already exists and already downloads all 18 countries; the incremental intake work is registering the other 17 countries' rows in `requirements.yaml` (mechanical) and deciding the country-dimension schema question (§4.2, a real but small design decision). The larger cost is downstream: extending L1 to a hierarchical multi-country model is a modeling project with its own validation, and it needs its own holdout discipline sealed before any fitting (§4.3) — neither of those is "free" just because the data is.
- **The license wrinkle, which is the header finding of this note:** JST is CC BY-NC-SA — non-commercial. `docs/data/LICENSE-REGISTRY.md`, whose own title is "what needs clearing before commercial use," currently misclassifies `jst` as `FREE` with "no licence needed for commercial use." That's wrong and should be corrected regardless of what campaign-4 decides. Substantively: JST backing *research validation* (battery diagnostics, held-out-regime backtests, L1 fit quality checks) is squarely inside its license and is exactly what the platform's own design docs already scoped it for. JST backing a model component whose **output ships to players in a commercial product** is a different question this note cannot resolve — the CC BY-NC-SA ShareAlike clause plausibly extends the non-commercial restriction to derivative works, and whether a generator trained partly on JST-derived priors counts as a "derivative" in that sense is a licensing judgment call, not an engineering one. **UNVERIFIED and out of this note's scope: whether the JST authors would grant a specific commercial-use permission on request — this is not researched here and would need direct contact with the Lab, not another web search.**

**Recommendation (the owner's call, framed, not made):**

- **GO, cheaply:** register JST's other 17 countries in `requirements.yaml` for **research use** — L1 hierarchical partial-pooling experiments, RQ2 held-out-regime backtests, and L2 crisis-chronology cross-validation. This reuses the existing connector, costs little, produces real (not reconstructed) data, and stays inside JST's license as currently scoped by the platform's own documents.
- **NO-GO, as literally framed:** "L3 neural generator trained cross-country on JST with a country embedding, generating US-conditioned decades" does not survive the frequency problem (§2) without either redefining L3 into a different, annual-grained layer (a real but large separate design effort) or reconstructing monthly data from annual JST, which the K3 rule already forbids. If campaign-4 wants more real monthly training volume for L3 specifically, JST is not the lever; Option D (§2) — new monthly cross-country connectors from IMF/OECD/national sources — is, and that is its own scoping note.
- **Immediate, independent of the above:** fix `docs/data/LICENSE-REGISTRY.md`'s `jst` classification (§1), and route the "can JST-trained model components ever back the commercial play surface" question to the owner as its own decision point — before, not during, any campaign-4 training run that touches JST-derived priors.

## 7. Explicit non-goals

- This note proposes **no seal change**. `factors.yaml`, `pre-registration.yaml`, and `governance/amendment-log.yaml` are read here, not written.
- This note runs **no training, no fetch, no compute**. Every number above is either quoted from a cited external source or read from a committed repo file; nothing was executed.
- This note does **not** decide the campaign-4 go/no-go. It is one of the two "cheap research probes" `NEXT-STEPS.md` §2 names as preconditions, not the decision itself.
- This note does **not** resolve the commercial-license question in §6 — that is flagged as an open owner (and possibly counsel) decision, not answered here.
- This note does **not** design the `uk`-block connectors in `Instructions/WP1.12-UK-CONNECTORS.md` — it points to that file and summarizes what `factors.yaml` and the decision register already say about it, without opening or re-scoping that document's own content in detail.
