# Step 5 in plain English

*Companion to `KICKOFF-STEP5.md` and `STEP5-DECISION-EVALUATION-PLAN.md`.
The explanation, not the authority — where they disagree, the plan wins.*

## What Step 5 is for

Everything until now built a world and the experience of living through
it. Step 5 answers the question the platform exists to answer: **were the
decisions any good?** Not "did they work out" — luck decides that — but
"given what was knowable at the time, across everything that could have
happened next, was that a good call?" It is the difference between
judging a poker player by one hand's winnings and judging them by how
they played the cards they could see.

## The seven pieces

**1. The scoring machine.** The rulebook was already sealed (we froze it
mid-Step 4, before any outcome existed, precisely so nobody could tune
the test after seeing answers). This piece makes it run: every
decision-maker scored on identical worlds with identical dice, compared
against fair benchmarks — a plain 60/40 portfolio, a classic endowment
mix, simple rules of thumb — with proper statistics that always report
how big an effect is, never just whether it exists.

**2. The picture engine.** A long-standing IOU: one command that turns
any recorded run into a page of charts — the world, the portfolio, the
reported-versus-true toggle, the episode annotations. Every figure in
every eventual paper comes from this one pipeline, regenerable from a
run's ID by anyone.

**In parallel: the generator retraining** (you said schedule it now).
One retrain fixes the known blemishes — crashes too shallow, regimes too
brief — and adds the missing ingredients: foreign currency, the
valuation signal, and the high-yield channel the 2022 replay told us
about. It needs a machine with a serious graphics card; final research
numbers wait for it, everything else proceeds meanwhile.

**3. The road not taken, computed.** Every run gets its do-nothing twin.
And for any moment of decision, the engine can regrow the whole fan of
futures from exactly that point — so "was it a good call?" becomes an
actual calculation over the thousands of decades that could have
followed, not a story told with hindsight.

**4. The tournament.** Same decade, same dice, many players. Built from
birth for all three of your formats: solo, the month-long real-time
cohort, and everyone-in-a-room simultaneous play. Leaderboards, full
exportable minutes, and honest dispersion statistics.

**5. The big study — pilot first (your call).** *Where in a decade do
decisions matter?* We start with hundreds of worlds rather than
thousands, with the meter running visibly: the pilot's report ends with
a cost-per-world table so the full version gets budgeted from data, not
guesses. The expected finding — that consequence concentrates at the
start, at regime breaks, and in the nerve-racking re-risking window
after a crash — gets tested, not assumed.

**6. Spending the sacred data — with your hand on the key.** The slice
of real history locked away since Step 2 gets used exactly once, at the
very end, as final confirmation. This step never happens on autopilot:
it waits for your explicit go, every time, no standing instruction
covers it.

**7. The honest write-up.** One document assembling all the research
questions — including the parked practitioner study (waiting for the
polished product, as you decided) and every negative result. With the
deepest caveat printed in the paper itself: a strategy that wins in our
simulator has been tested against our simulator; the two cannot be fully
separated, and saying so is what makes the rest believable.

## What you decided at kickoff

Retrain the generator now; park the practitioner panel until the product
shines; pilot the big study at hundreds of worlds with costs measured;
consent for research use designed in from day one; and the play formats
in your order — solo, then the real-time month, then live multiplayer.
