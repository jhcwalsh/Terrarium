# WP2.9 bake-off — companion measurements

`bakeoff.md` / `bakeoff.json` are generated by `scripts/run_sampler_bakeoff.py`.
This file records the measurements taken with the other WP2.9 commands, so the
generated table is not the only evidence. **Nothing here is a gate**, and
nothing here fed a selection: every number below was measured *after* both arms'
sealed selections were closed.

Both arms: campaign vintage `2026-07-26.1`, block dataset 367 raw overlapping
L=6 blocks (247 train raw, ~42 effective per epoch, validation folds 35/35/35,
15 straddling dropped) — byte-for-byte the WP2.8 dataset, so "same data" is a
fact rather than an intention.

## 2. How much the auxiliary actually drove each selection

The sealed text requires reporting both S terms with their scales precisely
because a fixed λ weights them differently per arm. Measured over each arm's own
completed trials:

|  | 3a (31 trials) | 3b (40 trials) |
|---|---|---|
| gen sd | 0.0524 | 0.1822 |
| aux sd | 0.1107 | 0.2078 |
| sd ratio aux/gen | **2.11×** | **1.14×** |
| corr(S, aux) | 0.911 | 0.644 |
| corr(S, gen) | 0.489 | 0.489 |
| argmin S == argmin aux | yes | **no** |
| argmin S == argmin gen | no | no |

So λ = 1.0 made 3a's selection *auxiliary-dominated* and 3b's *genuinely joint* —
3b's winner is the best on neither term alone. This is the limitation the seal
names, quantified per arm rather than asserted.

## 4. Guidance: it buys conditioning adherence and it costs tail fidelity

The sealed criterion selected `guidance_scale = 1.0` — guidance OFF — but with
`cond_dropout = 0.2`, so the checkpoint carries a *trained* null branch and the
same weights can be sampled guided. Ablation arms, same checkpoint, same folds,
same seeds:

| guidance | aux (D4 FZ) | NFE/block | `dw_equity_cum_log` | `dw_spread_center_pct` | `h_spread_level_pct` | regime one-hot |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 (selected) | **−3.409** | 4 | 69% | 19% | 15% | 3% |
| 1.5 (ablation) | −3.302 | 8 | 96% | 25% | 20% | 5% |
| 2.5 (ablation) | −3.118 | 8 | 144% (overshoot) | 32% | 30% | 8% |

Percentages are the finite-difference model slope as a fraction of the
historical OLS slope of the same relation. **Classifier-free guidance raises
every conditioning channel and worsens the D4 tail auxiliary monotonically**, at
double the sampling cost. That is a real trade the sealed criterion cannot see
the good side of — it scores `gen + 1.0·aux` and nothing else — and it is
reported here rather than folded silently into either direction. It is also
*learned aim, not repair*: guidance amplifies conditioning the network already
reads, unlike the inference-time level correction `ig-spread-diagnosis.md` §7
remedy 4 proposes, which overwrites the network's output and is in the same
category as Denton.

## 5. Conditioning response vs WP2.8's measurement

`bakeoff.md` carries the full table. Headline: **both trained samplers are
conditioning-attenuated on the same channels, in the same order, and neither is
uniformly better.** 3a is stronger on `dw_equity_cum_log` (78% vs 69%),
`state_pi_star` (46% vs 30%) and `dw_log_cpi` (39% vs 28%); 3b is stronger on
`dw_spread_center_pct` (19% vs 14%) and the regime one-hot (3% vs 2%) — the two
channels the `ig_spread` diagnosis identified as the worst. **The regime→spread
channel remains at 2–3% of its historical strength in BOTH families**, which
makes it a property of the frozen `cb-v1` contract and the ~42-effective-block
training set, not of either objective. `dw_policy_rate_pct` has a negative
historical slope on this panel and both models respond positively to it —
recorded as a sign disagreement, not as a ratio.

Method note: this table's regime row uses train-segment band centres
(`build_dataset`'s split-hygiene stats) where `scripts/diagnose_ig_spread.py`
used whole-panel stats. Identical for both arms, so the cross-arm comparison is
unaffected; a direct read-across to the diagnosis' printed numbers is not.

## 7. Batching evidence for the 3b checkpoint (WP2.8b requirements (i)–(vii))

`scripts/verify_block_batching.py --arm hier-flow-v1` (real checkpoint,
synthetic L1/L2, no catalog):

* **width 1 == the per-block path, bit for bit** — asserted structurally by
  `tests/test_blocks_flow.py::TestBatchedSampling::
  test_end_to_end_width_1_reproduces_the_unbatched_driver`, which drives one
  ensemble through the batched driver at width 1 and another through a sampler
  that does not advertise `sample_blocks` at all, and compares with
  `assert_array_equal`.
* **cross-width divergence, 20 decades × 120 months, CPU** (width 1 reference):

  | width | wall s | worst factor gap | worst gap / that factor's sd |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 1 | 6.3 | — (reference) | — |
  | 8 | 2.7 | 2.76e−05 (`equity_vol`) | 9.2e−06 (`mom`) |
  | 128 | 5.6 | 2.76e−05 (`equity_vol`) | 9.2e−06 (`mom`) |

  Same order as WP2.8b measured for 3a (worst 1.8e−05 of sd). Float32 GEMM
  round-off, not behaviour; never asserted equal.
* **row independence VERIFIED, not assumed**, and the WP2.8b claim corrected in
  the process: at a fixed width a row kept at its own batch INDEX is exact
  (isolate it with every other row zeroed and it reproduces the full batch bit
  for bit), but moving it to another index shifts it by ~2.4e−07. Chunking maps
  decade *m* to index *m mod width* in every run, so the property the acceptance
  filter needs holds exactly. Both families, CPU and CUDA. See
  `CHANGELOG.md` and `TorchBlockSampler`'s docstring.
* **the whole integration loop is wrapped**, not one step; **tracing stays at
  batch 1**; **`block_batch` and `device` are in the ensemble lineage** — the
  same code, inherited, so these are 3a's guarantees unchanged rather than
  re-implemented ones.

## 8. Throughput at a declared width and device

Block sampling only (`run_sampler_bakeoff.py`, 2,048 blocks, width 128, CUDA):

| arm | NFE/block | blocks/s | s/decade | s per 10k decades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3a | 31 | 537.7 | 0.0744 | **744** |
| 3b (selected) | **4** | **4346.2** | **0.0092** | **92** |
| 3b @ guidance 1.5 | 8 | 2038.3 | 0.0196 | 196 |
| 3b @ guidance 2.5 | 8 | 1381.6 | 0.0290 | 290 |

End to end through the joinery (`verify_block_batching.py`, 64 decades × 120
months, width 128, CUDA — includes L1 simulate, L2 regimes, waypoints, Denton,
support and cross-fade, all still per-decade and single-threaded):

| arm | wall s | s/decade | s per 10k decades | speed-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3a | 10.6 | 0.166 | ~1,656 | 1.0× |
| 3b | 2.3 | 0.036 | ~359 | **4.6×** |

The 8.1× advantage on block sampling becomes 4.6× end to end, exactly as
WP2.8b predicted: at these widths the bottleneck has moved to the CPU-side
joinery. **For WP2.10's budget, 3b removes roughly three quarters of the neural
sampling cost of a battery run**, which is the constraint WP2.8's cost finding
flagged (≥3 seeds × 2 samplers × filtered/unfiltered).
